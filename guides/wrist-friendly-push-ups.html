The Problem: Wrist Pain Kills Your Push-up Progress

You start a push-up routine. Two weeks in, your wrists ache. By week three, every rep hurts. You search online and find the same advice: "just push through it" or "use push-up handles."

Neither solves the root problem: standard push-ups place extreme extension pressure on the wrist joint. For people with existing wrist issues, carpal tunnel, or limited mobility, this isn't just uncomfortable — it's a one-way ticket to injury.

⚠️ Wrist pain is the #1 complaint among bodyweight beginners. The weight is manageable. The form is learnable. But wrist discomfort stops people before they even get started.

Shoulder Safety: The Other Silent Killer

Dips, pull-ups, and overhead pressing all demand significant shoulder mobility and stability. Flared elbows, shrugged shoulders, and excessive range of motion are the three most common form errors — and all three lead to rotator cuff issues over time.

IronLog includes built-in safety cues for every exercise: scapular depression before pull-ups, controlled descent for dips, neutral wrists for push-ups.

Quick Wrist-Friendly Modifications

  1. Fist push-ups — Make fists instead of flat palms. Keeps wrists neutral.
  2. Parallettes/dumbbells — Grip handles to keep wrists straight.
  3. Incline push-ups — Higher angle = less wrist pressure.
  4. Knee push-ups — Reduce total load while you build wrist tolerance.
  5. Wall push-ups — The gentlest option. Build up gradually.

Shoulder-Friendly Pull-up Checklist

  1. Depress your scapulae first — pull shoulders down and back BEFORE you pull. This engages the right muscles and protects the rotator cuff.
  2. Don't flare elbows — keep them at roughly 45° from your body, not straight out to the sides.
  3. Full range with control — go all the way down, but don't "drop" into the bottom position. Controlled negative = stronger tendons.
  4. If you can't do one rep — use negatives (jump up, lower slowly) or band-assisted pull-ups. Don't force it.
