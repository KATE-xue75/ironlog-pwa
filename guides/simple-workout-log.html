The Problem: Fitness Apps Have Become Social Networks

Open a modern fitness app and what do you see? An algorithm-driven feed. Friend requests. Challenges. Leaderboards. Push notifications begging for engagement. Stories. Streaks. Badges.

Somewhere along the way, workout trackers stopped being about working out. They became yet another screen to scroll, another inbox to manage, another source of notification anxiety.

📱 The average fitness app sends 5-10 push notifications per day. "Your friend just finished a workout!" "Don't lose your streak!" "Try this new challenge!" — for an activity that should be about focus and discipline, not engagement metrics.
